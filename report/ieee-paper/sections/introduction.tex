Graphs are widely used to model complex relational data in 
domains such as social networks, bioinformatics, and chemistry. 
Applying standard machine learning methods to graphs is challenging 
due to their irregular structure. Graph embedding techniques address 
this by mapping graphs into low-dimensional vector spaces that 
preserve structural information, enabling tasks such as classification 
and clustering.

Several embedding methods exist with different strengths. 
Graph2Vec captures subgraph patterns to produce graph-level embeddings efficiently. 
NetLSD encodes graphs via spectral descriptors, providing compact and 
calable representations. Graph Isomorphism Networks (GIN) use neural architectures 
to aggregate neighborhood information, achieving high predictive performance but 
at higher computational cost.

In this study, we evaluate these three methods on IMDB-MULTI, ENZYMES 
and MUTAG, comparing accuracy, robustness, scalability, and computational 
efficiency. The results highlight the trade-offs of each method and provide 
guidance for selecting appropriate embeddings based on dataset characteristics 
and task requirements.
